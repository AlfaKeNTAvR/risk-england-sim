Timeline pace

The cavalry starts on the 5th century. After each player's turn, that player rolls a die: on a 6 the cavalry moves 1 space. After every full round it moves 1 space. Ignore the printed dice gates. Reaching the 15th ends the game.
Why: the printed timeline gives about 3 turns each and seat 1 wins twice as often as seat 4. This gives about 6 turns and fair seats.

Defender sees the roll

Before a battle, move the attacking troops across, leaving at least 1 behind. The attacker rolls first. The defender looks, then picks 1 or 2 dice. Ties go to the defender. Winners occupy; no extra troops move in until your manoeuvre.
Big attacks barely change; close fights favour the defender.

Rivers and mountains

Only the manoeuvre is affected, never attacks. Crossing a river: at most 5 troops, and they stop on the far side. Entering a mountain territory: they stop there.
Rivers: Leeds–Beeston, Leeds–Bolsover, Leeds–Conisbrough, Portchester–Donnington, Donnington–Farnham, Farnham–Jewel Tower, Jewel Tower–Dover, Colchester–Dover.
Mountains: Carlisle, Brougham, Cliffords, Beeston, Wall, St Briavels, Old Sarum.

Rulings

Power card losses hit only the player who flips them; Heavy snowfall hits only your North territories. No territory drops below 1. For "lose 2" or "lose 3", use a territory that can take the full loss if you have one, else your largest.
Jewel Tower mission: 3 turns in total, anyone's turns count.
Winning: most territories when the cavalry reaches the 15th.
From 20,000 simulated games: trade cards early, at 3 to 6 stars. Hoarding to 10 wins only about half as often.
Flipping power cards pays off: you choose where losses land, and the good cards are strong.
