Medieval England House Rules

Timeline pace tested

  1. The cavalry starts on the 5th century.
  2. After each player's turn, that player rolls a die. On a 6 the cavalry moves 1 space.
  3. After every full round, the cavalry moves 1 space. Ignore the printed dice gates.
  4. When it reaches the 15th century, the game ends. Most territories wins.
Why: with the rulebook timeline, each player gets about 3.5 turns, and because the game ends mid-round, seat 1 wins 34% of games and seat 4 only 17%. Our pace gives about 6 turns each, and every seat wins about 25%.

Defender sees the roll tested

  1. The attacker rolls their dice first.
  2. The defender looks at the result, then chooses to roll 1 or 2 dice (2 only with 2+ troops).
  3. Compare as usual. Ties go to the defender.
Effect: a 3-against-2 roll becomes an even trade (0.75 troops lost each, instead of 0.92 attacker / 1.08 defender). Well-prepared attacks barely change: 21 against 10 still wins 96%. Close fights flip: 11 against 10 drops from 57% to 45%. It rewards preparation and punishes gambles.

Rivers and mountains

These only affect the manoeuvre at the end of your turn, never attacks.

  • Crossing a river: at most 5 troops per turn, and they stop on the far side until your next turn.
  • Entering a mountain territory: the troops stop there until your next turn.

River crossings

North: Leeds–Beeston, Leeds–Bolsover, Leeds–Conisbrough. South: Portchester–Donnington, Donnington–Farnham, Farnham–Jewel Tower, Jewel Tower–Dover, Colchester–Dover. Moving through Jewel Tower to High Wycombe, Berkhamstead, Colchester or Donnington is not a crossing.

Mountains

Carlisle, Brougham, Cliffords, Beeston, Wall, St Briavels, Old Sarum.

Cards

Territory cards (rulebook, confirmed)

Draw 1 card for every territory you conquer. This is the official rule, not a house rule.

Tip from the simulator: cards are the biggest troop source in this edition (about 85 troops per player per game). Trading at 3 to 6 stars wins about 30% of games; hoarding until 10 stars wins only 16%.

Power cards

  • Loss cards hit only the player who flips them. Heavy snowfall hits only your own North territories.
  • A territory never drops below 1 troop.
  • For "lose 2" or "lose 3", pick a territory that can take the full loss if you have one. Otherwise take it from your largest territory.
Tip from the simulator: flipping wins 31% against 19% for never flipping. Losses stay small because you choose where they land.

Missions

"Capture and hold Jewel Tower for 3 turns": 3 turns in total, and every player's turn counts.

Alliance pacts not yet simulated

  1. Sign: two players agree a length in turns, at least 1 turn each. Each puts the same number of territory cards face up on the pact board: 1 card for 1-2 turns, 2 cards for 3-4 turns.
  2. During: the two may not attack each other. A player can be in one pact at a time.
  3. Kept: when both have taken their agreed turns, each takes back their cards plus bonus cards from the deck: pact length minus 1, at most 3.
  4. Renew: allowed straight away, with the same player or anyone else.
  5. Broken: the moment one attacks the other, the victim takes the whole pile. On that turn the betrayer gets at most 1 territory card in total. The betrayer becomes an oathbreaker and can't sign a pact until the cavalry moves to the next century.
Watch for: cards are worth more than we assumed, so pact bonus cards may be too generous. This is the next thing to simulate.

Century events not yet simulated

Each time the cavalry enters a new century, including the 5th at the start, roll a die: 1-2 is the first event, 3-4 the second, 5-6 the third. The effect lasts until the cavalry moves again. A century skipped by a 6 has no event. "Troops" are placed by the holder; losses never take a territory below 1.

Or roll one century at a time.
