Centuries

  1. Who starts: everyone rolls a die; highest goes first, re-roll ties. Deal the territory cards as the rulebook says (the players going last get any extra). The cavalry starts on the 5th century, and the first player opens it.
  2. Opening a century: whoever's turn moves the golden cavalry opens the next century. They roll its event, mark the event with the yellow cavalry, and stand one of their own cavalry on the century.
  3. Moving on: after each player's turn, that player rolls a die. On a 6 the cavalry moves 1 space. Otherwise it moves when play comes back round to the opener, so a century lasts at most one full circle. Ignore the printed dice gates.
  4. The end: the 14th century ends at the end of a round (after the last player in turn order), or on a 6. Reaching the 15th ends the game at once. Most territories wins.

Defending one die at a time

  1. Before a battle, move the attacking troops across, leaving at least 1 behind. Only those troops fight; winners occupy the territory.
  2. The attacker rolls their dice first.
  3. The defender rolls one die and looks at it. With 2 or more troops, they may then roll a second die, or stop at one.
  4. Compare as usual. Ties go to the defender.

Rivers and mountains

Manoeuvring

  • Crossing a river: at most 5 troops per turn, and they stop on the far side until your next turn.
  • Entering a mountain territory: the troops stop there until your next turn.

Attacking

  • Climbing and fording: when you attack into a mountain territory or across a river, remove 1 of your committed troops before the first roll. It's once per battle, however many rolls it takes.
  • A new attack on the same territory, after you withdraw, costs 1 again.
  • Leeds to Beeston is both a river and a mountain: it costs 2.
  • So commit at least 2 troops: a single climber never reaches the fight.

River crossings

North: Leeds–Beeston, Leeds–Bolsover, Leeds–Conisbrough. South: Portchester–Donnington, Donnington–Farnham, Farnham–Jewel Tower, Jewel Tower–Dover, Colchester–Dover. Moving through Jewel Tower to High Wycombe, Berkhamstead, Colchester or Donnington is not a crossing.

Mountains

Carlisle, Brougham, Cliffords, Beeston, Wall, St Briavels, Old Sarum.

Cards

Territory cards (rulebook, confirmed)

Draw 1 card for every territory you conquer. This is the official rule, not a house rule.

Power cards

  • Loss cards hit only the player who flips them. Heavy snowfall hits only your own North territories.
  • A territory never drops below 1 troop.
  • For "lose 2" or "lose 3", pick a territory that can take the full loss if you have one. Otherwise take it from your largest territory.

Missions

"Capture and hold Jewel Tower for 3 turns": 3 turns in total, and every player's turn counts.

Alliance pacts

There are two pacts in the game, purple and orange. A player can be in one pact at a time.

  1. Sign: two players agree a length of 1, 2 or 3 turns each and take a treaty card. Each places territory cards face up under their side of it, worth exactly as many stars as the pact lasts: 1 star for 1 turn, 2 stars for 2 turns, 3 stars for 3 turns. If you can't make the exact amount, you can't sign.
  2. During: the two may not attack each other. Each signer stands one of their cavalry on their number of turns on the treaty card. At the end of each of your turns, move it one space on.
  3. Kept: when both cavalry reach “kept”, each takes back their stake plus bonus cards from the deck: pact length minus 1, so at most 2. Renewing straight away is fine.
  4. Broken: the moment one attacks the other, the victim takes both stakes. The betrayer draws territory cards as normal that turn, but takes an oathbreaker seal: no pacts until the cavalry moves.

A handshake truce without a stake is still allowed, as in normal Risk. It just isn't binding.

Century events

Each century is an era: its event changes how the game works for as long as the cavalry stays there. When a century opens, the opener rolls a die: 1-2 is the first event, 3-4 the second, 5-6 the third. Mark it with the yellow cavalry. Effects marked At once happen when the century begins; everything else lasts the whole century. Every century happens: a 6 just moves the cavalry on early, so that century is shorter. Losses never take a territory below 1 troop.

Events escalate and lean against the leader. Early centuries bring boons and small shocks, the middle ones change how fighting works, and late ones break up the leader's position.

The leader is the player with the most territories at that moment (on a tie, the tied player earliest in turn order). Defect: the territory switches to the player with the fewest territories; remove its troops and place 1 of the new owner's troops there. No card is drawn for it. Thinnest means fewest troops; on a tie, the owner picks.

The 15th century has no events: reaching it ends the game.

Or roll one century at a time.
